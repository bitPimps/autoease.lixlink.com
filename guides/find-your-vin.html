Where to find your car’s VIN (and what it means)

Your VIN is on the driver’s door jamb, the dashboard by the windshield, and your registration, title and insurance card. Here’s where to look and how to read it.

Updated November 14, 2026 · By the AutoEase team

Quick answer

Open the driver’s door and look at the sticker on the door jamb, or look through the windshield at the driver’s-side bottom corner of the dashboard. Your VIN is the 17-character code there. It’s also on your registration, title and insurance card.

Where to find your VIN

A VIN (vehicle identification number) is your car’s unique 17-character ID. It’s printed in several places, so you can usually find it without opening the hood:

  1. Driver’s door jamb. Open the driver’s door and look at the sticker on the edge of the door or on the frame where the door latches. It usually has the VIN as text and as a barcode. This is the barcode AutoEase’s scanner reads.
  2. Dashboard, through the windshield. Stand outside the car and look through the windshield at the bottom corner on the driver’s side. A small metal plate shows the VIN.
  3. Vehicle registration card. Your state registration lists the VIN.
  4. Insurance card or policy. Most auto insurance cards and apps show it.
  5. Title. The certificate of title always includes the VIN.

Check that the VIN on the car matches your paperwork. If they don’t match, ask your dealer or DMV.

How to read a VIN

Since the 1981 model year, every car, truck and SUV sold in the US has a 17-character VIN. Each part means something:

CharactersWhat they tell you
1–3World manufacturer identifier: the country and the company that built the car.
4–8Vehicle descriptor: model, body style, engine and other details, coded by the manufacturer.
9Check digit: a number calculated from the others, used to catch fake or mistyped VINs.
10Model year (for example, M = 2021, N = 2022, P = 2023, R = 2024, S = 2025).
11Assembly plant.
12–17Serial number of the individual vehicle.

VINs never use the letters I, O or Q, because they look too much like 1 and 0. If you think you see an O, it’s a zero.

Why your VIN matters

  • Safety recalls. NHTSA’s recall lookup uses your VIN to show open recalls on your exact car.
  • The right parts. Shops use it to order parts that fit your engine and trim.
  • Insurance and registration. It’s how your car is identified on official records.
  • Setting up AutoEase. AutoEase sends your VIN to NHTSA’s free vPIC decoder to look up the year, make and model, then sets up your maintenance schedule. Nothing else about you goes with it.

Common questions

Where is the VIN on my car?

The easiest places are the sticker on the driver’s door jamb and the small plate on the dashboard at the bottom corner of the windshield on the driver’s side. It’s also on your registration, title and insurance card.

How many characters is a VIN?

17 letters and numbers for every car, truck and SUV sold in the US since the 1981 model year. VINs never use the letters I, O or Q.

Is it safe to share my VIN?

A VIN identifies a car, not a person, and it’s visible through the windshield of every parked car. It’s routinely shared with insurers, shops and buyers. Keep your registration and title documents private.
