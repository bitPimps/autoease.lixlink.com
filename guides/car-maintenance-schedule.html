How often to service your car: a plain-English schedule

Typical intervals for oil changes, tire rotations, brakes, filters and more, what “whichever comes first” means, and when to follow your owner’s manual instead.

Updated November 20, 2026 · By the AutoEase team

Quick answer

For most modern cars: oil change every 5,000–10,000 miles or 6–12 months, tire rotation every 5,000–7,500 miles, brakes inspected about once a year, engine air filter every 15,000–30,000 miles. Whichever comes first, miles or months. Your owner’s manual has your car’s exact schedule.

“Miles or months, whichever comes first”

Most services have two limits: a distance and a time. Oil breaks down with age as well as use, and rubber and fluids age even when a car sits. So a service is due when you hit either limit. If you drive little, the months will usually come first.

Do you drive in “severe” conditions?

Many manuals have a second, shorter schedule for severe service. It often applies to ordinary driving: mostly short trips, stop-and-go traffic, towing, dusty roads, or very hot or cold weather. If that sounds like you, use the shorter intervals.

Weather affects your car too

Tire pressure drops as the temperature falls, roughly 1 psi for every 10°F, so a cold snap can leave tires under-inflated overnight. Extreme heat is hard on batteries and the cooling system. Check tire pressure when it turns cold, and coolant and battery before a heat wave.

Common questions

How often should I change my oil?

Most newer cars using synthetic oil need an oil change every 5,000 to 10,000 miles or 6 to 12 months, whichever comes first. Check your owner’s manual or your car’s oil-life monitor for your exact interval.

Is the 3,000-mile oil change still necessary?

For most modern cars, no. The 3,000-mile rule dates from older engines and oils. Follow your owner’s manual, and use its severe-service schedule if you mostly make short trips, tow, or drive in extreme heat or cold.

How often should tires be rotated?

Typically every 5,000 to 7,500 miles, which is why many people do it with each oil change.

What if I don’t drive much?

Time limits still apply. Oil and fluids age even when a car sits, so a car driven little is often due by months before miles.
